The **Workflow Copilot** drafts a workflow with you in conversation and then proves the draft
on one of your own issues with a **dry run**, before anything is published. It edits the same
draft the [Workflow Studio](./studio.mdx) and the [code view](./code.mdx) edit.

## The Copilot page

Open a workflow and choose **Copilot** in the **Visual** · **Code** · **Copilot** row, or go to
`/workflows/<slug>/copilot`. **Workflows** stays selected in the sidebar: the three are views
of one workflow, not three places.

<Screenshot id="user-guide.workflows.copilot" />

The three views show the **same draft**. Change a stage on the canvas, switch to **Copilot**,
and the page is working from the changed draft. Nothing is lost by switching, with one
exception that asks you first: leaving **Code** while the file does not parse — see
[Workflows as code](./code.mdx#saving-and-the-round-trip-with-the-canvas).

The page head has two actions, **Discard draft** and **Promote draft → `<slug>` vN**.

Under the head are four cards: [the conversation](#the-conversation) on the left, and
[the draft's stage list](#the-draft), [the dry-run card](#the-dry-run-card) and
[**Why dry runs are safe**](#why-dry-runs-are-safe) on the right.

While the page loads, each card is drawn as an outline in its own shape, so nothing moves when
the content arrives. If something on the page is not as you expect, see
[What can go wrong](#what-can-go-wrong).

### Starting a workflow from a conversation

Choose **+ New workflow** in the rail. Under **Start with**, pick **Copilot** instead of
**Canvas**, then give the workflow its **Name** and **Slug** as usual. The new, empty workflow
opens on its Copilot page rather than on the canvas. Opened from a Copilot page, the dialog
already has **Copilot** selected.

### Promoting a draft

**Promote draft → `<slug>` vN** publishes the draft as the workflow's next version, for
example **Promote draft → security-patch v1**. It is the same publish the
[Studio](./studio.mdx#publishing-a-version) does, with the same checks.

Before anything is published, a confirmation says **What is being published**: the version,
the draft it comes from, and each **guard** — a promise made about the workflow — with where
that promise is actually enforced:

| Guard | Comes from | Enforced |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **never auto-merge** | The workflow itself: no stage ends by merging on its own. | **Enforced on real runs.** |
| **spend cap $5.00/run** | Something you asked for in the conversation. | **Not enforced on real runs yet.** It limits [dry runs](#the-budget) of the workflow only. |

A guard marked **Not enforced on real runs yet** is not a protection once published, and the
confirmation says so. To cap what real runs spend, use the
[spend guard](../../administration/policies.mdx) in the workspace's policies.

If the checks find problems, nothing is published. Each finding names the stage it is about;
choose one to go to that stage's row in [the draft](#the-draft), which is outlined and brought
into view.

When a promote succeeds, runs queued from then on use the new version, and the conversation
that produced it is closed and kept **read-only**.

### Unresolved references

A draft can name things the workspace does not have yet — a skill, a task route or a model
alias. The page says so **before** you press Promote, under the buttons:

- **This draft cannot be promoted yet** — the draft names a **model alias** the workspace does
  not have. **Promote** is unavailable, each reference is listed as
  `stage <stage> · <name>`, and **Resolve them on the Visual surface** takes you to the canvas.
- **N unresolved references are flagged** — the draft names a **skill** or a **task route**
  that does not exist yet. These do not stop a promote; they are published as they are, and a
  stage that depends on one will not do its work until it exists.

A workflow with no draft has nothing to promote, and the button says that too.

### Discarding a draft

**Discard draft** asks **Discard this draft?** and, if you confirm, throws the draft away. It
cannot be brought back.

- The **workflow stays**. It returns to its published version; a workflow that was never
  published is left empty.
- The **conversation is kept, read-only**, so what was tried can still be read on the page.

A draft cannot be discarded while a dry run of it is still running. Wait for the dry run to
finish, or cancel it, and discard again.

### Who can promote and discard

Owners and admins. Everyone else sees the page read-only, with a note saying so: the buttons
are shown but unavailable, and a conversation can be
[read but not continued](#who-can-take-part).

## The conversation

The **Conversation** card is where you describe the workflow and the Copilot edits the draft.
Every change it makes is an ordinary edit to the draft, the same kind the canvas and the code
view make. The card says so underneath: *Copilot edits compile to the same graph as the canvas
and the code editor — nothing is a special case.*

<Screenshot id="user-guide.workflows.copilot.conversation" />

The Copilot replies through the invocation gateway, which calls the model its
`copilot-workflow` route resolves to — see
[What a chain does when a model call fails](../models.mdx#what-a-chain-does-when-a-model-call-fails).

### The head of the card

- **The model pill** names the model the newest reply was actually routed to, for example
  `claude-fable-5`. Hover it to see the alias it was routed through. It is read from the
  conversation's own record, so there is no pill until the first reply has been recorded.
- **The draft tag**, for example `draft: security-patch`, names the draft the conversation
  edits.

### Messages

Your messages sit on the right and the Copilot's on the left. Each has a line above it saying
who spoke and when, in your own time zone: **You · 15:02**, **Copilot · 15:02**.

A reply can contain **bold** text, `inline code` and blocks of code. Nothing else is
formatted; anything else in a reply is shown as the plain text it is.

The conversation scrolls inside its card. It opens on the newest 30 messages; **Show earlier
messages** adds the 30 before them without moving the message you were reading.

### Watching a reply arrive

A reply appears as it is written. As the Copilot changes the draft, each change is listed in
the reply at the point it happened, in small type:

| Marker | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `adding stage exploit-verify… ✓` | A stage was added. Updating and removing a stage read the same way. |
| `connecting test → exploit-verify… ✓` | Two stages were connected. `disconnecting … ✓` removes a connection. |
| `set trigger… ✓` | The workflow's trigger was changed. |
| `set spend cap $5.00/run… proposed, not applied` | A guard was recorded. It is **not** part of the draft; see [Promoting a draft](#promoting-a-draft) for where a guard is enforced. |
| `⚠ exploit-verify: …` | The change named something the workspace does not have yet. See [Unresolved references](#unresolved-references). |
| `read catalog…` | The Copilot read something before answering: the draft, the stage catalog, the skills or the issues. |
| `proposed a dry run on #489 — …` | The Copilot suggested an issue to [dry-run](#dry-runs) against. |
| `draft changed elsewhere — …` | The draft was edited on the canvas or in the code view during the reply, and the Copilot read it again. |

A `✓` means the change is in the draft.

While a reply is arriving the card follows it, unless you have scrolled up to read something.
Then it stays where you are and shows **New messages ↓**, which takes you back to the end.

When you come back to the page later, a reply's changes are folded into one line under its
text, such as **applied 6 operations · 1 corrected**. Choose it to see the list.

### Corrected operations

Sometimes a change the Copilot attempts is refused because it does not fit the draft, for
example connecting to a stage whose name it misspelled. The Copilot is told why and corrects
itself in the same reply. This is normal and needs nothing from you.

The card notes it with one quiet line, **1 operation corrected**. Choose the line to see what
was attempted and the reason it was refused.

### Questions and choices

When the Copilot would otherwise have to guess, it asks. Each question is a row of choices
under the reply; a reply with more than one numbers them ① ②.

Choose an option to answer. The answer is sent at once as your next message, the Copilot
carries on from it, and the option you chose is marked with a `✓`. An answered question
cannot be answered again: all of its choices become unavailable.

Only the newest reply with an open question can be answered, and not while a reply is
arriving or a dry run is in progress.

### Sending a message

Type in the box at the foot of the card — it reads *Refine the workflow — or ask for a dry
run…* — and press **Enter** or **Send**. **Shift+Enter** starts a new line.

What you have typed and not sent is kept for the workflow while the browser tab is open, so
you can switch to **Visual** or **Code** and come back to it.

The box is switched off, with the reason shown under it, when a message cannot be sent:

| Reason shown | When |
| --- | --- |
| *The copilot is replying — you can send again when the reply finishes.* | A reply is arriving. |
| *A dry run is in progress — the draft is held still until it finishes. You can send again then.* | A dry run of the draft has not ended. |
| *A reply is still arriving in this conversation, started from another page. Reload to read it, then send.* | A reply was started from another tab or by someone else. |
| *The copilot cannot reply: this deployment has no invocation gateway.* | See [When the Copilot cannot reply](#when-the-copilot-cannot-reply). |
| *The copilot cannot reply: no model is routed for it yet.* | See [When the Copilot cannot reply](#when-the-copilot-cannot-reply). |

If a message is refused before a reply starts, the reason is shown above the box and your
text is put back so you can send it again.

### Cost

A reply that was priced shows what the exchange cost at the end of its line:
**Copilot · 15:02 · $0.12**. A reply whose model has no price set shows no figure at all; it
is never shown as `$0.00`.

### An empty conversation

A workflow that has no conversation yet shows **Describe the workflow you want** and three
example briefs. Choosing an example puts it in the box; it is not sent until you send it. The
conversation starts with your first message.

Under the examples the card says plainly what to expect:

| The copilot will | It will not |
| --- | --- |
| Draft the stages from your brief, and ask when a choice is yours to make. | Run anything, write to a repository or open a pull request. |
| Change only this workflow's draft — you can see every change in the stage list. | Publish the workflow — that is **Promote draft**, and yours to press. |
| Show what each reply cost, when the model is priced. | Start a dry run by itself; you start one from the dry-run card. |

Someone who cannot start a conversation sees **No conversation yet** instead, without the
examples.

### When the Copilot cannot reply

Two things must be in place before the Copilot can answer. When one is missing, the card says
which, above the messages, and the box is switched off. Messages already in the conversation
stay readable.

- **The copilot needs the invocation gateway** — this deployment cannot call a model, and the
  Copilot cannot exist without one. Only a deployment of a REST release before 0.41.36 lacks it. This is a prerequisite, not an error: the page does not
  spin, and nothing fails when you press a button. The notice reports the workspace's current
  provider status — for example *Provider status: 4 connected — 3 active, 1 not yet checked.*,
  or *no provider is connected in this workspace* — and links to
  [Providers](../../administration/providers.mdx) through **See Providers**, where providers
  are connected and checked. The status line is left out if the providers cannot be read.
- **The copilot has no model route** — the workspace has no route for the Copilot's task kind,
  `copilot-workflow`, or the route does not resolve. Every workspace has the task kind; its
  route is created the first time an owner or admin saves a chain for it. The notice gives the
  reason and links to **Model routing** — see
  [Routing a task kind that has no route](../models.mdx#routing-a-task-kind-that-has-no-route).

### When a reply fails

A reply that could not be finished is marked **This reply did not finish**, with the reason.
Whatever it had already changed in the draft is kept. **Retry** sends your message again as a
new message; the conversation keeps both the reply that failed and the new one. **Retry** is
offered on the newest reply only.

If the draft is promoted or discarded while a reply is arriving, the reply stops and nothing
further is changed. The card marks it **The conversation was closed while this reply was
arriving**. There is nothing to retry.

### Who can take part

Owners and admins can send messages and answer questions.

Members and viewers see the same card read-only: the messages, the answered choices and the
costs, with no box. In its place the card says *This conversation is shown read-only:
continuing it edits the workflow, which is for workspace owners and admins.*

A conversation that was promoted or discarded is closed. Everyone can still read it, and the
card says which happened. An owner or admin can choose **Start a new conversation** to begin
again on the same workflow; the closed one is no longer shown once the new one has started.

## The draft

The card beside the conversation lists the draft's stages, in the order a run walks them. Its
title names the workflow — **Draft — security-patch · unpublished** — and says **unpublished**
only while the workflow has never been published.

<Screenshot id="user-guide.workflows.copilot.stages" />

The list is read from the draft itself, not from the conversation, so it is there for a
workflow nobody has talked to the Copilot about, and it is the same list for every role.

### Stage rows

Each row is one stage: its number, a mark for its kind (the same marks the
[canvas](./studio.mdx#stage-kinds) uses), its name, and its **config tags**.

Two stages with the same name and kind that follow the same stage are one step done twice, and
are shown as one row: **review ×2**, with both stages' tags.

A stage that nothing leads to from the trigger is still listed, after the others.

### Config tags

A tag is a setting of the stage, read from the draft each time the list is drawn. Nobody types
them, so they cannot drift from the stage:

| Tag | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| `label:security`, `effort ≤ M`, `source:github` | On the trigger: what an issue must match to start a run. One tag per condition. |
| `skill:advisory-db` | The skill the stage loads. |
| `claude-sonnet-5` | The model the stage is pinned to. The stage names a model alias; the tag shows the model that alias currently points at, or the alias itself if the workspace has no such alias. |
| `routed by task` | The stage is not pinned: its model comes from [model routing](../../administration/providers.mdx). |
| `pool-a` | The runner pool the stage runs on. |
| `twister full`, `sandboxed` | The stage's description. Parts separated by ` · ` become one tag each. |
| `never auto-merge`, `auto-merge`, `back to queue` | On the last stage: how the run ends. |

Change a stage on the canvas or in the code view and its tags follow. You do not need to
reload: the list re-reads the draft every 15 seconds while the tab is visible, and at once
when you come back to the window.

### Added by copilot

A row carries **added by copilot** when the Copilot added that stage. It is the draft's own
record of who added what, so it does not depend on the conversation still being open. A stage
added by applying a dry-run suggestion carries **added by suggestion** in the same way.

The mark is meant to clear once a person edits the stage: a stage you have reconfigured is no
longer the Copilot's.

:::info[Not available yet]

An edit made on the canvas or in the code view does not clear **added by copilot** yet. The
stage's tags do follow the edit; only the mark stays.

:::

### Warning tags

A stage can name a skill, a task kind or a model alias the workspace does not have yet. The
row does not present it as a working setting. Its tag is drawn as a warning, with `⚠` and what
is wrong in a few words:

| Tag | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `skill:advisory-db ⚠ skill does not exist` | No skill with that name exists in the workspace. |
| `task:exploit-verify ⚠ kind not in catalog` | The stage takes its model from a task kind that does not exist. |
| `fast-triage ⚠ alias does not exist` | The stage is pinned to a model alias that does not exist. |

Choose the **ⓘ** beside the tag — or Tab to it and press Enter — to read what is missing, what
resolves it, and the service's own message. Choose it again, or press Escape, to close it.

While any reference is unresolved, the stage that names it cannot do its work, and the foot of
the card says **draft not yet runnable on real issues**. Which references also stop a promote
is described under [Unresolved references](#unresolved-references) above.

### The revision

The tag beside the title, for example `draft v0.3`, is the draft's revision: the published
version the draft started from, then the number of batches of changes applied since. A
workflow that has never been published starts from `v0`. While a reply is arriving the number
moves with each batch the Copilot applies.

### Watching the draft build

The rows change while the Copilot is still replying. A stage appears the moment the Copilot
adds it, a row's tags change when its stage is changed, and rows move as stages are connected.
A row that has just been added or changed is briefly highlighted; if your system asks for
reduced motion, it changes without the highlight.

### Edit and View as code

- **edit**, at the end of each row, opens the [canvas](./studio.mdx#the-canvas) with that
  stage selected, centred, and open in the inspector. On a **×2** row it opens the first of
  the two stages. Everyone can follow it; whether you can change the stage there depends on
  your role.
- **View as code →**, at the top of the card, opens the same draft in the
  [code view](./code.mdx).

### The foot of the card

One line, all of it read from the draft:

- **How many stages** — rows, so a **×2** row counts once.
- **Each guard** a promote would carry, as the [promote confirmation](#promoting-a-draft)
  names it. A guard that is not enforced on real runs says so here too: `spend cap $5.00/run
  (not enforced on real runs yet)`.
- **Whether the draft could run**: **draft not yet runnable on real issues** while any
  reference is unresolved, otherwise **draft runnable once promoted**.

### An empty draft

A workflow with no draft, or a draft with no stages, shows **No stages yet** and a link,
**Open the canvas**. The stages appear as the conversation builds them, or as you add them on
the canvas.

### When the list cannot be refreshed

If a later read of the draft fails, the list stays as it was last read, under a note: **These
stages may be out of date**, with the reason and **Read again**. If the draft cannot be read
when the page opens, the page says **The draft's stages could not be read** with a **Retry**,
and the rest of the page still works.

A long list scrolls inside the card.

## The dry-run card

The card under the stage list shows the workflow's **newest dry run**: what each stage did,
the change the run drafted, the suggestions it led to, and the controls to run again. What a
dry run is, and what it does and does not do, is described under [Dry runs](#dry-runs) below;
this section describes the card.

<Screenshot id="user-guide.workflows.copilot.dry-run" />

### The head of the dry-run card

- **The title** names the issue the run tested: **Dry run — #489 CAN arbitration-lost storm
  under full telemetry load**. A long title is cut short; hover it to read the whole of it.
- **The pill** always reads `simulated — no writes · no PRs · no merges`. It is true of every
  dry run: see [Dry runs](#dry-runs).
- **The totals** are the time the run took and what it cost: `2m 41s · $0.31`. A run whose
  models have no price set shows the time alone; a cost is never shown as `$0.00`. While a run
  is going, the totals' place shows how long it has been running instead.

### Result rows

One row for each stage, in the order the run walked them:

```text
✓ analyze                                  mapped 4 files · advisory DB skipped (no CVE on this issue)
✓ implement       (simulated)              diff drafted +41 −9 (below) · 84k tokens
✓ build           (replayed from history)  est. 3m 42s (217 similar builds, ±22s)
⏸ exploit-verify  (skipped)                no PoC exists: stage had nothing to do
○ open PR                                  would open DRAFT PR · not merged (policy)
```

Each row has a mark, the stage's name, a label saying **how** the result was produced, and a
note in the run's own words.

| Mark | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `✓` | The stage finished. |
| `⏸` | The stage was skipped. Its label and note are shown in the warning colour. |
| `○` | The stage was not run. On the last stage it says what a real run would have done there, such as opening a draft pull request. |
| `✕` | The stage failed. |

The label in parentheses is the part to read before trusting a row:

| Label | What the row is |
| --- | --- |
| *(none)* | A real result: a model was called and this is what it did. |
| `(simulated)` | A model was called for real, and its edits were held in memory. Nothing was written to the repository. |
| `(replayed from history)` | **An estimate.** Nothing was built or run. The figure comes from similar past builds or test runs. |
| `(skipped)` | The stage did nothing, and the note says why. |

Two stages that are one step done twice share a row, as they do in the
[stage list](#stage-rows): **review ×2**.

### Where an estimate comes from

A `replayed from history` row has an **ⓘ** after its label. Choose it — or Tab to it and press
Enter — to see what the estimate rests on. Choose it again, or press Escape, to close it.

| Shown | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **estimate** | The estimated time, for example `3m 42s`. It is the median of the sample. |
| **sample** | How many similar past builds or test runs it is based on: `n = 217`. |
| **spread** | How much those varied: `± 22s`. |
| **window** | How far back the sample was taken from: `30-day window`. |
| **formula** | The formula used, by name and version, with one sentence saying what it computes: `formula build_duration_replay v1 — …`. |

The more past runs behind an estimate and the smaller its spread, the more it is worth. The
figures are taken from your own runners' history at the time of the dry run, so the same draft
dry-run a week later can show a different estimate.

#### Not enough history

When too few similar runs exist, the row shows **no figure at all** — not in its note and not
behind the ⓘ. The ⓘ says only what was found and what is needed:

> Not enough history: 12 similar builds in the last 30 days, 20 needed.

An estimate is given once the number needed has been reached.

### The simulated diff

Under the rows is the change the run drafted, as a diff. The block is headed, inside the
block itself, **simulated diff (never written to repo)**, followed by each file it touches
and its added and removed line counts: `drivers/can/arbitration.c +41 −9`.

It is the edit the workflow *would* make. No branch, commit or pull request was created for
it.

A long diff scrolls inside the block. A very large one is cut off, and the block then says
so underneath with both sizes: `Truncated — showing the first 64.0 KB of 212.4 KB.`

### Suggestions on the card

Each open [suggestion](#suggestions) is a highlighted callout under the diff, marked `✦`:
its title, what it is based on, and where it came from.

- **A suggestion from a rule** shows a confidence, such as `93%`, and under it the rule's name
  and version: `rule · skipped-nothing-to-do v1`. Choose the confidence to see how it was
  scored — the method and every number that went into it. See [Confidence](#confidence).
- **A suggestion from the Copilot's model** is labelled `from the model · unscored` and shows
  no percentage. See [Suggestions from the Copilot](#suggestions-from-the-copilot).

Each callout has three actions:

- **Apply to draft** opens a confirmation first. It lists every change the suggestion will
  make, in order — `adding stage has-cve`, `connecting test → has-cve` — each of which opens to
  the exact operation, and it names the revision the draft will become: *This makes draft
  v0.4.* Nothing is changed until you confirm. See [Apply to draft](#apply-to-draft).
- **Explain** opens the rule, its version, what it found in this run and how the confidence
  was scored. See [Explain](#explain).
- **Ignore** dismisses the suggestion. See [Ignore](#ignore).

After you confirm an apply, a notice at the top of the page reads **Applied — the draft is now
v0.4.** and lists the **Changed stages** as links. Choose one to go to that stage's row in
[the stage list](#the-draft), which has already been re-read. The callout is replaced by one
quiet line, *Applied — …*; an ignored suggestion leaves *Ignored — … (it will not be raised
again for this draft revision)*.

A suggestion that is advice only keeps its **Apply to draft** button, switched off, with the
line *This suggestion is advice only — it proposes no change to apply.*

If the draft has changed since the dry run so that the suggestion no longer fits, the
confirmation says which part no longer fits, changes nothing, and offers **Re-run dry run**.

### Re-run dry run and Dry-run another issue

At the foot of the card:

- **Re-run dry run** starts a new dry run on the same issue, against the draft **as it is
  now**. When the draft has moved on since the run on the card, a line under the buttons says
  which draft it will use: *re-runs on draft v0.4*. See [Re-running](#re-running).
- **Dry-run another issue ▾** opens a ranked list of issues under the buttons. Each entry has
  its rank, the issue, and **the reason it ranks where it does** — for example *Meets every
  condition — exercises the main path.* An issue that would not start this workflow is still
  listed, marked **would not trigger this workflow**: those are often the most useful ones to
  try. Choose **Dry-run #490** beside an issue to start. How the ranking works is under
  [Choosing an issue](#choosing-an-issue). Press Escape to close the list.
- **The history line** — `history: 1 dry run · draft v0.3 (2 copilot edits)` — says how many
  dry runs the workflow has had, the draft's current revision, and how many batches of changes
  the Copilot has applied to it.

Starting a dry run needs the workspace's GitHub token, as described under
[Starting a dry run](#starting-a-dry-run).

A dry run's model stages call a model through the invocation gateway, the same one the
[conversation](#when-the-copilot-cannot-reply) replies through, each with its own routing.

### Watching a dry run

A dry run takes minutes. While one is going, the card follows it without a reload:

1. Each stage appears as a row marked `◌` and **running…** when it starts, and fills in when
   it finishes.
2. When the run ends, the totals replace the running time and the simulated diff appears.
3. Then the suggestions appear.

**Cancel dry run** is shown while a run is going. See [Cancelling](#cancelling). **Re-run dry
run** and **Dry-run another issue** are switched off until it ends, with the reason: *One dry
run at a time — this one is still running.* The [conversation's](#sending-a-message) message
box is switched off for the same time.

If the live connection is lost, the card says so and reads the run again every few seconds
instead; nothing is missed.

### How a run's end is shown

A run that ends **complete** shows its rows, diff and suggestions with nothing added. Every
other end has a box above the rows, and the rows the run reached are kept under it:

| Box | When | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **This dry run failed** | A stage failed. | The reason, as the run recorded it. |
| **The draft did not pass the pre-check** | The [structural pre-check](#the-structural-pre-check) found a problem, so no stage ran. | Each finding. A finding about a stage is a link to that stage's row in [the stage list](#the-draft). |
| **This dry run was stopped at its budget** | The [budget](#the-budget) was reached. | What stopped it, what it had spent, the cap and whether it was the workspace's or the draft's own spend guard, and what to change before running again. |
| **This dry run was cancelled** | Someone cancelled it. | Nothing more. |
| **A guard had to stop this dry run** | A stage tried something a dry run must never do. See [Guards](#guards). | Each attempt that was blocked: what was tried, which guard refused it, how many times, and in which stage. |

**A guard had to stop this dry run** is drawn more heavily than the others, in the error
colour, and is not an ordinary failure. Nothing was written, but do not rely on that run's
results, and find out why the stage tried it before promoting the draft.

### When a dry run cannot start

If a dry run is refused, the reason is shown at the top of the card, in a box you can
**Dismiss**. The previous run's results stay on the card underneath.

| Heading | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **A dry run of this workflow is already running** | Someone else started one. The card reads it and follows it. |
| **The draft changed before the dry run could start** | The draft was edited after the page was read. The page reads it again; start the dry run once more. |
| **That issue has no repository to read** | The issue names no repository mirrored for the workspace. |
| **That issue's tracker cannot trigger a workflow** | The issue comes from a source a workflow's trigger cannot name. |
| **The dry-run engine is not available** | The service that runs dry runs could not be reached. |
| **The dry run did not start** | Anything else — for example, the workspace has no GitHub token to read the repository with. The sentence under the heading says what. |

### Before the first dry run

A workflow that has never been dry-run shows **No dry run yet** and **Choose an issue ▾**,
which opens the same ranked list.

If the Copilot proposed an issue in the conversation, the card names it with the Copilot's
reason — *The copilot suggested dry-running #489 — security workflow × no-CVE issue —
exercises the skip path* — and offers **Dry-run #489**. If that issue is not in the ranked
list, the list opens instead and says so.

### Who can use the card

Owners, admins and **members** can start, cancel and re-run a dry run, and apply or ignore a
suggestion. This is wider than [the conversation](#who-can-take-part), which only owners and
admins can continue.

Viewers see the same card — the rows, the diff, the suggestions, **Explain** and the
confidence — with every other control switched off and the reason beside it.

A dry run also cannot be started while the draft has no stages.

If the dry run cannot be read when the page opens, the page says **The dry run could not be
read** with a **Retry**, and the rest of the page still works.

## Dry runs

A dry run walks a workflow's draft for one issue, the way a loop would, with three
differences that make it safe to run on a draft:

| What happens | In a dry run |
| --- | --- |
| Model stages | Run for real, with the draft's own prompts, skills and routing. This is what a dry run tests. |
| Edits to the repository | Held in memory as a **simulated diff**. Nothing is written, pushed or opened as a pull request, and nothing is merged. |
| Build and test stages | **Replayed from history**: an estimate from similar past builds and test runs, with how many it is based on and how much they varied. Nothing is built or run. |

It is not the Studio's [**Dry run**](./studio.mdx#dry-runs), which walks the graph without
calling a model and costs nothing. A dry run here calls models and has a cost, so it has a
budget.

A dry run is kept apart from your loops. It is not a run, and its cost is not counted in any
run, dashboard or spend figure.

The page states these three differences itself, and shows what enforces each: see
[Why dry runs are safe](#why-dry-runs-are-safe).

### Starting a dry run

A dry run needs three things:

- **A draft.** The dry run tests the workflow's saved draft at the revision you are looking
  at — `v0.3`, for example. If the draft has changed since you opened it, the dry run is
  refused and you are asked to reload.
- **An issue.** An issue from a GitHub, GitLab, Jira or Linear source that names a GitHub
  repository mirrored for the workspace. An issue that names none is refused, because the dry
  run would have nothing to read.
- **The workspace's backlog token**, the GitHub token the repository is read with. Without one
  the dry run is refused. See
  [Setting the backlog token](../../administration/sources.mdx#setting-the-backlog-token).

Owners, admins and members can start, cancel and re-run a dry run. Viewers can read them.

A workflow has **one dry run at a time**. Starting a second while the first is still going is
refused; follow the first or cancel it.

When a dry run starts, Ouroboros notes the newest commit on the repository's default branch.
Every stage reads the repository as it was at that commit, so an edit made while the dry run
is going does not change what it sees.

### The structural pre-check

Before any model is called, the draft is checked for structural problems — a stage nothing
leads to, a branch with no condition, a trigger that is not valid. These are the same checks
[**Validate**](./studio.mdx#validation) runs.

If the check finds a problem, the dry run ends at once as **failed**, lists each finding
against the stage or edge it is about, and **calls nothing** — no model, no estimate and no
repository read, so it costs nothing. Fix the draft and start again.

A stage that names a skill, a task route or a model alias that does not exist yet is not a
structural problem. The dry run still runs: a stage whose model cannot be resolved is
**skipped** with the reason, and a stage whose skill does not exist runs without it and says
so.

### The budget

A dry run stops spending at its **budget**, which is the lower of two caps:

- **The workspace's dry-run cap** — `$10.00` unless your administrator has changed it. See
  [The dry-run cap](../../administration/policies.mdx#the-dry-run-cap).
- **The draft's own spend guard** — a per-run cap set on the draft in conversation with the
  Copilot, such as *cap spend at $5 a run*. A draft with none has only the workspace's cap.

For example, a draft with a `$5.00` spend guard in a workspace capped at `$10.00` has a budget
of `$5.00`. A dry run says which of the two caps set its budget.

A run stopped at its budget says what to change:

- stopped by **the draft's own spend guard** — ask the Copilot to raise the draft's per-run
  spend cap, or make the model stages cheaper, then re-run;
- stopped by **the workspace's dry-run cap** — make the draft's model stages cheaper, or ask a
  workspace owner to raise the cap, then re-run.

### How a dry run ends

A dry run reports each stage as it starts and as it finishes, in order. It ends in one of
four ways:

| Ends as | Meaning | What is kept |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **complete** | Every stage that could run did. | Every row, the simulated diff, the totals. |
| **budget stopped** | The budget was reached. | The rows reached so far; the rest are marked not reached. |
| **cancelled** | Someone cancelled it. | The rows reached so far. |
| **failed** | The pre-check found a problem, a stage failed, or a guard blocked a call. | The findings, or the rows reached so far, with the reason. |

Each finished stage shows what happened and **how the result was produced**: a real model
call, **simulated** (a model stage whose edits were held in memory), **replayed from history**
(an estimate), or **skipped** with the reason.

The totals are the time taken, the tokens used and the cost. A cost is left blank when the
models used have no price set; it is never shown as `$0.00`.

#### Guards

Stages in a dry run can only read the repository, draft edits in memory, and ask for a build
or test estimate. If a stage tries anything else — running a command, pushing, opening a pull
request, reaching the network — the attempt is blocked and recorded.

A dry run that had to block an attempt **fails**, even if every stage finished. Its result
lists what was attempted and by which stage. A clean dry run lists nothing.

### Cancelling

Cancelling stops a dry run that is still going. The rows it had reached are kept, and it ends
as **cancelled** — not failed, and not budget stopped. A dry run that has already ended cannot
be cancelled.

### Re-running

A re-run starts a new dry run against the **same issue** and the draft **as it is now** — not
the revision the first run tested. Change the draft, re-run, and the new dry run shows what
the change did. The first run stays in the history beside it.

A re-run does everything a new dry run does: it notes the newest commit, runs the pre-check
and works out the budget again.

### History

Each workflow keeps its dry runs, newest first. The history says how many there have been and
which draft is current, for example `history: 1 dry run · draft v0.3 (2 copilot edits)`. Each
entry shows the draft revision and issue it tested, how it ended, and its totals, so two runs
of the same issue before and after an edit can be compared.

The history also gives the total the workflow's dry runs have cost.

### Choosing an issue

A dry run is most useful on an issue that does **not** fit what the workflow expects, because
that is the issue that shows what the workflow's conditions do. Ouroboros ranks the
workspace's open, sized issues for a draft and gives a reason for each.

It looks at everything in the draft that tests the issue:

- each condition of the trigger — every required label, the largest effort, the source;
- each stage or branch that tests the issue's labels, effort or source.

An issue scores one point for each of these it does **not** meet. The highest score comes
first; equal scores go to the issue updated most recently.

For example, for a draft triggered by the label `security`:

| Rank | Issue | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `#489` — labels `bug`, `can-bus` | no `security` label (trigger) — outside the trigger, so it exercises the path a matching issue would not. |
| 2 | `#550` — labels `ota`, `security` | Meets every condition — exercises the main path. |

The Copilot uses the same ranking when it suggests an issue to dry-run against.

## Why dry runs are safe

Under the dry-run card, **Why dry runs are safe** states what a dry run does and does not
touch, in up to three columns:

<Screenshot id="user-guide.workflows.copilot.safety" />

| Column | What it says | What makes it true |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Simulated writes** | Diffs are drafted in memory — the repo is untouched. | The **in-memory overlay**: a stage's edits are held in memory, later stages read through them, and they become the simulated diff. No tool a dry run has can write to a repository. |
| **Replayed infra** | Build & test are estimated from your history; the farm stays idle. | The **replay estimator service**: build and test stages are answered from your past builds and test runs, with a sample count and spread. Nothing is dispatched to the build farm. |
| **Real models** | Prompts & routing are exercised for real — that's what you're testing. | **Invocation gateway routing**: every model stage runs its own prompt through the gateway with its routing resolved, counted against the dry run's budget. |

Press the **ⓘ** beside a column to see its mechanism: what it is, what it does, the dry-run
tools that carry it, the path it goes through, and — for **Replayed infra** — the formulas the
estimate uses. One is open at a time; press it again, or **Esc**, to close it. The tag in the
card's head names the dry-run harness the claims are about.

### A column that is missing

The columns are not fixed text. The page reads them from the service, which checks each one
against the dry-run harness that is installed, and a column is shown **only while its mechanism
is in place**.

- If one or two are missing, the card says so in a highlighted line — *1 of this strip's 3
  claims is not shown: the installed dry-run harness does not back it, so the page does not
  make it.* — and the missing column is not drawn at all.
- If none is in place, the card shows only a warning: do not rely on a dry run being safe
  until this is resolved.

Treat a missing column as a real loss of that guarantee and tell your administrator.

If the claims cannot be read, the page says **The dry run's safety claims could not be read**
with a **Retry**, the card makes no claim, and the rest of the page still works.

## Suggestions

When a dry run ends **complete** or **budget stopped**, Ouroboros reads its result rows and
files **suggestions**: proposed changes to the draft, each one worked out from what the run
recorded. A dry run that failed or was cancelled gets none.

A suggestion has a title, a sentence saying what it is based on, the exact change it would
make to the draft, and three actions: **Apply to draft**, **Explain** and **Ignore**.

### The rules

Suggestions come from a fixed set of rules. A rule either finds what it looks for in the run
or says nothing; it does not guess.

| Rule | Fires when | Proposes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `skipped-nothing-to-do` | A stage was skipped because it had nothing to do on this issue, and named the label the issue lacks. | A decision ahead of the stage, so issues without that label go around it. |
| `budget-proximity` | The run spent at least 80% of its budget, or stopped at it. | Raising the cap or trimming the costliest stage, with the run's own figures. Advice only. |
| `unresolved-reference-persists` | A stage was skipped because a skill, task route or alias it names does not exist, and the draft still names it. | The closest name the workspace does define, when one is close. Otherwise advice only. |
| `reviewer-disagreement` | Review replays exist for the workflow, at least five pairs were replayed, and at least half disagreed on style — more than on substance. | Loading the `pr-etiquette` skill into both reviewer stages. Advice only when the workspace has no such skill. |

For example, a `security-patch` draft dry-run against `#489`, which has no `cve` label, skips
its `exploit-verify` stage with nothing to check. The first rule proposes a `has-cve` decision
ahead of that stage: issues labelled `cve` go through it, the rest go straight to review.

:::info[Not available yet]

`reviewer-disagreement` needs review replays, which Ouroboros does not record yet. Until it
does, this rule never fires and no such suggestion appears.

:::

A suggestion that is **advice only** proposes no change to the draft. It can be explained and
ignored, and its **Apply to draft** button is switched off, with the reason.

### Confidence

Each rule's suggestion shows a confidence from 0 to 100. It is always
**rule strength × sample adequacy**, rounded to a whole number:

- **Rule strength** is how firmly what the rule saw implies what it proposes.
- **Sample adequacy** is how much evidence there was. One dry run against one issue counts as
  `0.98`. Review replays count as `1 − 1/n` for `n` replayed pairs.

| Rule | Rule strength | Sample adequacy | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `skipped-nothing-to-do` | `0.95` | `0.98` | 93 |
| `budget-proximity` | `0.9` | `0.98` | 88 |
| `unresolved-reference-persists` | `0.95` | `0.9` when a close name is proposed, `0.98` otherwise | 86 or 93 |
| `reviewer-disagreement` | `0.9` | `1 − 1/n` | 81 for 10 pairs |

Both numbers are stored with the suggestion, together with what the rule counted, and
**Explain** shows them.

### Suggestions from the Copilot

If the dry run was started from a Copilot conversation, the Copilot is shown the results when
the run ends and may add suggestions of its own. These are labelled `from the model ·
unscored` rather than with a rule's name, and they show **no confidence**: the change they
propose is checked against the draft before it is kept, but it is not scored.

The Copilot's reply appears in the conversation as its next message. It does not repeat a
suggestion a rule has already made.

### Apply to draft

**Apply to draft** first shows [what it will change](#suggestions-on-the-card). When you
confirm, the suggestion's change is made to the draft in one step. The draft moves to its next
revision, for example from `v0.3` to `v0.4`, and the change is recorded as coming from a
suggestion. [Re-run](#re-running) the dry run to see what the change did: in the
example above, `exploit-verify` is now skipped because the decision routes `#489` around it.

The change was worked out for the draft as it was when the dry run ended. If the draft has
been edited since, **Apply to draft** checks the change against the draft as it is now:

- If the edit did not touch what the suggestion changes, it is applied.
- If it did — the stage or connection the suggestion relies on is gone, or the decision it
  adds already exists — nothing is changed, and you are told which part no longer fits. Run
  the dry run again to get suggestions for the current draft.

A suggestion can be applied once. Owners, admins and members can apply; viewers cannot.

### Explain

**Explain** shows how a suggestion was reached: the rule and its version, when that rule
fires and what it proposes, what it found in this run (the stage, the counts, the issue), and
the two confidence numbers with the inputs behind them. For a Copilot suggestion it shows the
model alias that proposed it and that it is unscored.

### Ignore

**Ignore** dismisses a suggestion. It stays in the dry run's record as ignored, and the same
rule does not make the same suggestion again on later dry runs of the **same draft
revision**. Once the draft changes, a new dry run may suggest it again.

An ignored suggestion cannot be applied afterwards.
