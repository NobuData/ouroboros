Chat Ops brings Ouroboros into Slack. This page covers three things:

- [the Chat Ops page](#the-chat-ops-page) in Ouroboros — how Slack is connected, and which
  channels it posts to;
- [what Ouroboros posts](#what-ouroboros-posts) to your channels — the questions loops are
  waiting on, finished loops and the weekly digest;
- [linking your Slack identity](#why-you-link) to your Ouroboros account, which you do once.

Your workspace's Owner or Maintainer connects Slack first and decides which channel gets what —
see [Chat Ops — connecting Slack](../administration/chatops.mdx).

:::info[What works today]

Ouroboros posts cards to Slack; linking, unlinking and the `/ouro` commands work — see
[Commands](#commands); and a card's buttons answer the decision — see
[Answer a decision from Slack](#answer-a-decision-from-slack). An unanswered decision escalates
when its countdown ends.

:::

## The Chat Ops page

The Chat Ops page is at `/chatops`. It is not in the sidebar. Reach it from:

- **Chat Ops →** on the **Slack** tile, in the **Integrations** section of
  <UiPath path="Settings" />;
- **Chat Ops** beside **Answer from anywhere** in the [Needs-you inbox](./inbox.mdx);
- **Weekly digest in Slack →** at the top of [Insights](./insights.mdx);
- the **Chat Ops** link on the Slack row of [Get Started](./getting-started/wizard.mdx).

<Screenshot id="user-guide.chatops" />

The page refreshes itself every few seconds. Every member can read it; binding channels,
installing and verifying are for an Owner or a Maintainer.

### The connection strip

Under the heading, one line says how the Slack connection stands — such as
**acme-robotics · app v2.4 · scopes ✓ · active · verified 2m ago**:

| Part | It means |
| --- | --- |
| The first name | The Slack workspace that is connected. |
| **app v2.4** | The version of the Slack app that was installed. |
| **scopes ✓** | Slack granted everything the app asks for. Otherwise it reads, for example, **1 scope missing: groups:read** — re-install the app to grant it. |
| **active** | Slack accepts the connection. |
| **revoked** | The app was removed from the Slack workspace, or its token was revoked. Slack's reason follows, such as **revoked — app_uninstalled**. Nothing reaches your channels until the app is installed again. |
| **error** | Slack is answering with an error, such as **error — ratelimited**. Messages may not reach your channels. |
| **verified 2m ago** | When Ouroboros last checked the connection with Slack. **not verified yet** means it has not. |

An Owner or a Maintainer also sees **Verify now**, which checks the connection with Slack at
once, and — when the connection is **revoked** or in **error** — **Re-install**. Everyone else
is told that an Owner or a Maintainer can re-install the app.

### Open the channel in Slack

**Open #ouroboros-loops ↗** at the top right opens your workspace's **Loops** channel in
Slack, in a new tab. It carries that channel's own name. The button is there only when a
channel has been bound for Loops; until then there is nothing to open, so it is not shown.

### Bound channels

**Bound channels** lists each channel Ouroboros posts to: its name, what it is for
(**Loops**, **Alerts** or **Leads**), how many members it has, and which notification routes
point at it. **Open ↗** opens that channel in Slack.

### Add a channel

An Owner or a Maintainer binds a channel with **+ Add channel ▾**:

1. Choose **+ Add channel ▾**. **Add a channel** lists the Slack workspace's channels. Each
   row says whether it is private, how many members it has, and whether **bot is a member** or
   **bot is not in this channel**.
2. Choose a channel, then say what it is for:

   | Purpose | Binding it switches on |
   | --- | --- |
   | **Loops** | Blocking questions, with a DM to the on-call person · loop finished · stage transitions (off) |
   | **Alerts** | Build failures |
   | **Leads** | The weekly digest |

3. Choose **Bind**. The page confirms, such as **#ouroboros-loops is bound for Loops.**
   Choose another channel, or **Done**.

**Ouroboros can only post where it is a member.** If the row reads **bot is not in this
channel**, open the channel in Slack and send `/invite @Ouroboros`, then choose **Refresh from
Slack** in the picker. Private channels are added the same way: the bot cannot join a channel
on its own. Binding a channel the bot is not in is refused, with that explanation.

Each purpose has one channel. Binding a second channel for **Loops** moves Loops to it.
**Unbind**, on a bound channel, stops Ouroboros posting there: the routes that pointed at it
are left without a channel.

**Manage in Settings →** at the foot of the page opens the **Notifications** section, where
each route has its switch — see
[Notifications, email & webhooks](../administration/notifications-and-webhooks.mdx).

### When Slack is not connected yet

The page then explains what is missing instead:

- **This deployment has no Slack app yet** — whoever operates the deployment has to register
  one first.
- **Install the Slack app into your workspace** — the deployment has an app, and an Owner or
  a Maintainer can install it with **Install**.

Both link the setup guide. The steps are in
[Chat Ops — connecting Slack](../administration/chatops.mdx).

## What Ouroboros posts

Every message is a **card**: a headline, the facts, sometimes a line of figures, and buttons.
The coloured bar on its left says what kind of news it is — green for done, amber for a
question, red for a failure.

### A question a loop is waiting on

When a loop reaches a decision only a person may make, Ouroboros posts it as a card:

- The **headline** is the question, and the line under it says why it is being asked. These
  are the same words the [Needs-you inbox](./inbox.mdx) shows for that decision.
- The next line says what it is about — such as **loop #1851 · issue #479 ·
  boot/rollback_flag.c**.
- The **buttons** are that decision's own actions. Buttons that answer it — such as
  **Allow once** and **Deny** — answer it as you; see
  [Answer a decision from Slack](#answer-a-decision-from-slack). Buttons that open a page — such
  as **View diff →** — open it in Ouroboros.
- The last line says how long the loop has waited and when the decision escalates — such as
  **paused 4m · escalates to the Needs-you inbox in 26m**. The times are as they stood when the
  card was last written; the card does not tick, but it is rewritten when the decision
  escalates or is answered.

A workspace may also send each question as a direct message to the person on call.

### The same card, once it is answered

When the decision is answered — in Slack, in the inbox, by email or anywhere else — Ouroboros
**edits the card in place**. Its buttons disappear and its last line says how it ended and who
ended it, such as **Maya Chen answered: allowed once · in Slack**. A decision that was settled
elsewhere reads **Closed — settled elsewhere**.

A short line then follows in the channel, such as
**✓ Maya Chen answered: allow once — loop #1851**.

So a card in a channel is never a stale invitation: if it still has answer buttons, the
decision is still open.

## Answer a decision from Slack

Press a button on the card — such as **Allow once** or **Deny**. Ouroboros does exactly what
the same button in the [Needs-you inbox](./inbox.mdx) does, as you: the loop resumes or is
sent back, the answer is recorded under your name with **in Slack** as where it came from, and
the audit log records it. You get a message only you can see, such as
**Answered: Allow once — exception granted · resume sent to loop #1851**, and the card closes
as described above.

**Who may press a button.** You must have [linked your Slack identity](#link-your-identity),
and your account must be allowed to give that answer — the same rule as in the inbox. Most
answers need the `can_approve_loops` capability; see
[Roles](../administration/roles.mdx). If you are not allowed, Ouroboros tells you — only you —
what the button needs, such as *Allow once needs the `can_approve_loops` capability*, and
nothing changes.

**If someone answered first.** In a busy channel two people can press within seconds. The
first press answers; the second does nothing, and its presser is told who answered — such as
*This was already answered — Maya Chen answered: allowed once · in Slack*. The card is edited
to its answered form for everyone.

**Merges are confirmed in Ouroboros.** A card that asks you to approve a merge has no answer
buttons. It has one button, **Confirm in Ouroboros ↗**, which opens the decision in the inbox
where you answer it signed in, and it says why: *A merge is confirmed in Ouroboros, signed in
— it cannot be answered from chat.* `/ouro answer` refuses a merge the same way.

**Answers that need a note.** Some answers come with a note — such as returning a plan to
the loop with what to change. Slack's button cannot take one, so pressing it answers, to you
alone, that the answer *needs a note, which a Slack button cannot take*, with an **Open in
Ouroboros** button. Give the answer there.

**A button that does nothing.** If a button answers *That button is not valid for this
message*, the press did not come from the card Ouroboros posted — nothing was changed. Open
the decision in the inbox instead.

### When nobody answers

Each decision has an escalation window — 30 minutes for most kinds. The card counts it down
from when the question was asked. If the window ends with no answer:

- the card's last line changes to **⚠ escalated — now in the Needs-you inbox**, so the channel
  can see it was escalated, not ignored;
- the decision moves to the top of the [Needs-you inbox](./inbox.mdx#time-limits-and-escalation);
- the person on call for decisions gets the card as a direct message, if someone is on call
  and has linked their Slack identity.

The buttons keep working after an escalation. Answering a decision, snoozing it in the inbox,
or its loop no longer needing it stops the countdown: the decision then never escalates.

### A loop that finished

When a loop's pull request merges, the card reads **Loop #1846 finished — PR #512 merged**,
with the issue under it, a line of figures, and **View PR ↗** and **Run console** buttons.

The line of figures reads like **14/14 checks · 11m cycle · $0.94 · claude-fable-5**. Each part
appears only when it is known:

- **The cost appears only when all of the loop's usage was priced.** If any of it was not,
  there is no cost on the card at all — never `$0.00`, and never an estimate.
- The model appears only when one is on record.

### The weekly digest

Once a week Ouroboros posts the week's figures from [Insights](./insights.mdx): how many pull
requests merged and how many needed a person, each headline figure against the week before,
where loops most often needed a person, flaky tests that moved, and the cost. It is the same
digest the weekly email carries, so the two always agree. **Open Insights** opens the page.

### Not posted yet

Cards for a build that went green or failed are not posted yet.

## Why you link

A button pressed or a command typed in Slack acts with **your** Ouroboros permissions and
appears in the audit trail under **your** name. A Slack display name can be changed by anyone,
so Ouroboros never trusts one. It acts only for a Slack identity that a signed-in person has
confirmed is theirs.

## Link your identity

1. In Slack, type `/ouro link`.
2. Ouroboros answers with a message only you can see, and a **Link your account** button.
   The link works once, for fifteen minutes, and only for you.
3. Choose the button. A page opens in your browser. If you are not signed in to Ouroboros it
   reads **Sign in to link your Slack identity** — choose **Sign in to continue**, and you
   return to the page afterwards.
4. The page reads **Link this Slack identity to your account?** It names your Slack identity
   and its workspace, and the Ouroboros account it will act as. Check both.
5. Choose **Link this Slack identity**.

The page then reads **Your Slack identity is linked**, and you can go back to Slack.

**Nothing is linked until you press the button.** Opening the link — or a mail scanner or
link preview opening it for you — changes nothing.

### The email note on the confirmation screen

The screen says whether the email on your Slack profile matches your Ouroboros account. This is
a hint, not proof: a match never links anyone by itself, and no match does not stop you.
Confirm only if the Slack identity shown is yours.

## Unlink

In Slack, type `/ouro unlink`. Ouroboros answers that your identity is unlinked. What you did
while it was linked stays in the audit trail under your name.

To link again, type `/ouro link`.

## Commands

Once you are linked, type `/ouro` and a command in any channel or direct message. The answer is
shown to you alone. <Since version="0.41.25" module="ouroboros-rest" />

| Type | To |
| --- | --- |
| `/ouro status` | see loops live, the queue, the build farm, today's spend and what needs you, in one line |
| `/ouro queue #485` | send an issue into the loop |
| `/ouro pause loop 1847` | hold a loop — `/ouro resume loop 1847` lets it continue |
| `/ouro abort loop 1847 confirm` | stop a loop for good |
| `/ouro answer q-231 allow_once` | answer a question that is blocking a loop |
| `/ouro dry-run #486` | rehearse a workflow with no writes and no pull requests |
| `/ouro route implement claude-fable-5` | repin the model a task kind uses |
| `/ouro estimate #490` | see an issue's effort, cost and cycle estimate |
| `/ouro pause all confirm` | pause every loop in the workspace |
| `/ouro help` | list the commands |

Each command does what the matching button in Ouroboros does, with your role: a command your
role does not include answers with who it is for, and does nothing. `abort loop` and
`pause all` run only when you add the word `confirm`. Without it they describe what would
happen.

The full reference — every argument, who may run each command and what it answers — is
[/ouro chat commands](../cli/ouro.mdx).

## If you press a button before linking

Ouroboros answers, to you alone, *You're not linked* with a **Link your account** button, and
does nothing else. Link, then press the button again.

## When a link does not work

| The page reads | What happened | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **This link is not valid** | The link was already used, was replaced by a newer one, or its address is incomplete. | Type `/ouro link` again. |
| **This link has expired** | More than fifteen minutes passed since it was sent. | Type `/ouro link` again. |
| **You are not a member of that workspace** | You are signed in to an account that is not a member of the Ouroboros workspace this Slack workspace is connected to. | Sign in with the right account, or ask an Owner or Maintainer to invite you. |
| **Your account already has a Slack identity** | Your account is already linked to another Slack identity in that Slack workspace. | Type `/ouro unlink` from the other identity first. |
| **Slack is no longer connected** | The Slack app was removed since the link was sent. | Ask an Owner or Maintainer to connect Slack again. |

Each of these pages ends with **Nothing was linked.**

If `/ouro link` answers *Your Slack identity is already linked*, there is nothing to do.
