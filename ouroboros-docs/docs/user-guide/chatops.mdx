Chat Ops brings Ouroboros into Slack. This page covers two things:

- [what Ouroboros posts](#what-ouroboros-posts) to your channels — the questions loops are
  waiting on, finished loops and the weekly digest;
- [linking your Slack identity](#why-you-link) to your Ouroboros account, which you do once.

Your workspace's Owner or Maintainer connects Slack first and decides which channel gets what —
see [Chat Ops — connecting Slack](../administration/chatops.mdx).

:::info[What works today]

Ouroboros posts cards to Slack, and linking works: `/ouro link`, `/ouro unlink` and
`/ouro help`. **Answering a decision with a button in Slack is not available yet** — the button
replies that it is not available and changes nothing — and neither is any other `/ouro` command.
Until then, answer decisions in the [Needs-you inbox](./inbox.mdx); a card's link buttons take
you to the right page.

:::

## What Ouroboros posts

Every message is a **card**: a headline, the facts, sometimes a line of figures, and buttons.
The coloured bar on its left says what kind of news it is — green for done, amber for a
question, red for a failure.

### A question a loop is waiting on

When a loop reaches a decision only a person may make, Ouroboros posts it as a card:

- The **headline** is the question, and the line under it says why it is being asked. These
  are the same words the [Needs-you inbox](./inbox.mdx) shows for that decision.
- The next line says what it is about — such as **loop #1851 · issue #479 ·
  boot/rollback_flag.c**.
- The **buttons** are that decision's own actions. Buttons that answer it — such as
  **Allow once** and **Deny** — are not available in Slack yet. Buttons that open a page — such
  as **View diff →** — open it in Ouroboros.
- The last line says how long the loop has waited and when the decision escalates — such as
  **paused 4m · escalates to the Needs-you inbox in 26m**. These times are as they stood when
  the card was posted; the card does not count down.

A workspace may also send each question as a direct message to the person on call.

### The same card, once it is answered

When the decision is answered — in Slack, in the inbox, by email or anywhere else — Ouroboros
**edits the card in place**. Its buttons disappear and its last line says how it ended and who
ended it, such as **Maya Chen answered: allowed once · in Slack**. A decision that was settled
elsewhere reads **Closed — settled elsewhere**.

A short line then follows in the channel, such as
**✓ Maya Chen answered: allow once — loop #1851**.

So a card in a channel is never a stale invitation: if it still has answer buttons, the
decision is still open.

### A loop that finished

When a loop's pull request merges, the card reads **Loop #1846 finished — PR #512 merged**,
with the issue under it, a line of figures, and **View PR ↗** and **Run console** buttons.

The line of figures reads like **14/14 checks · 11m cycle · $0.94 · claude-fable-5**. Each part
appears only when it is known:

- **The cost appears only when all of the loop's usage was priced.** If any of it was not,
  there is no cost on the card at all — never `$0.00`, and never an estimate.
- The model appears only when one is on record.

### The weekly digest

Once a week Ouroboros posts the week's figures from [Insights](./insights.mdx): how many pull
requests merged and how many needed a person, each headline figure against the week before,
where loops most often needed a person, flaky tests that moved, and the cost. It is the same
digest the weekly email carries, so the two always agree. **Open Insights** opens the page.

### Not posted yet

Cards for a build that went green or failed are not posted yet.

## Why you link

A button pressed or a command typed in Slack acts with **your** Ouroboros permissions and
appears in the audit trail under **your** name. A Slack display name can be changed by anyone,
so Ouroboros never trusts one. It acts only for a Slack identity that a signed-in person has
confirmed is theirs.

## Link your identity

1. In Slack, type `/ouro link`.
2. Ouroboros answers with a message only you can see, and a **Link your account** button.
   The link works once, for fifteen minutes, and only for you.
3. Choose the button. A page opens in your browser. If you are not signed in to Ouroboros it
   reads **Sign in to link your Slack identity** — choose **Sign in to continue**, and you
   return to the page afterwards.
4. The page reads **Link this Slack identity to your account?** It names your Slack identity
   and its workspace, and the Ouroboros account it will act as. Check both.
5. Choose **Link this Slack identity**.

The page then reads **Your Slack identity is linked**, and you can go back to Slack.

**Nothing is linked until you press the button.** Opening the link — or a mail scanner or
link preview opening it for you — changes nothing.

### The email note on the confirmation screen

The screen says whether the email on your Slack profile matches your Ouroboros account. This is
a hint, not proof: a match never links anyone by itself, and no match does not stop you.
Confirm only if the Slack identity shown is yours.

## Unlink

In Slack, type `/ouro unlink`. Ouroboros answers that your identity is unlinked. What you did
while it was linked stays in the audit trail under your name.

To link again, type `/ouro link`.

## If you press a button before linking

Ouroboros answers, to you alone, *You're not linked* with a **Link your account** button, and
does nothing else. Link, then press the button again.

## When a link does not work

| The page reads | What happened | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **This link is not valid** | The link was already used, was replaced by a newer one, or its address is incomplete. | Type `/ouro link` again. |
| **This link has expired** | More than fifteen minutes passed since it was sent. | Type `/ouro link` again. |
| **You are not a member of that workspace** | You are signed in to an account that is not a member of the Ouroboros workspace this Slack workspace is connected to. | Sign in with the right account, or ask an Owner or Maintainer to invite you. |
| **Your account already has a Slack identity** | Your account is already linked to another Slack identity in that Slack workspace. | Type `/ouro unlink` from the other identity first. |
| **Slack is no longer connected** | The Slack app was removed since the link was sent. | Ask an Owner or Maintainer to connect Slack again. |

Each of these pages ends with **Nothing was linked.**

If `/ouro link` answers *Your Slack identity is already linked*, there is nothing to do.
